How to hire a great freelance Website Designer

A website designer is a freelance professional who plans, designs, and builds the visual layout, user experience, and front-end of websites for businesses, brands, and individuals. Hiring a skilled website designer means getting a site that loads quickly, converts visitors into customers, and reflects your brand across every screen size.

What does a freelance website designer do?

A freelance website designer translates business goals into a working web presence. The role blends visual design, user experience (UX), and front-end implementation, often extending into content structure, conversion optimisation, and basic search engine optimisation. Some web designers specialise in pure visual mockups, while others handle the full build from wireframe to live site.

Commercially, the website is usually the first place a prospect goes after hearing about your business. A well-designed site builds trust, reduces bounce rates, and turns traffic into leads, bookings, or sales. A poorly designed one quietly costs revenue every day it stays live.

Services and deliverables a website designer provides

Scope varies by project, but a freelance web designer typically handles a defined set of deliverables across discovery, design, and launch.

  • Wireframes and sitemaps that map page structure and user flows
  • High-fidelity mockups in Figma, Adobe XD, or Sketch
  • Responsive design for desktop, tablet, and mobile breakpoints
  • Custom WordPress themes, Elementor builds, or block-editor layouts
  • Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, or Webflow site design and configuration
  • Landing pages built for paid traffic and conversion
  • Redesigns and migrations from legacy platforms
  • On-page SEO setup, schema markup, and Core Web Vitals optimisation
  • Accessibility (WCAG) compliance, contrast checks, and keyboard navigation
  • Style guides, design systems, and reusable component libraries

Tools and platforms website designers use

Most website designers work fluently across a stack of design tools and content management systems. The right mix depends on whether the project is a marketing site, an e-commerce store, or a custom web application.

  • Design tools: Figma, Adobe XD, Sketch, Photoshop, Illustrator
  • Content management systems: WordPress, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, Ghost
  • E-commerce platforms: Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce
  • Page builders: Elementor, Divi, Beaver Builder, Bricks
  • Front-end code: HTML5, CSS3, Tailwind CSS, JavaScript, React
  • Performance and SEO: Google PageSpeed Insights, Lighthouse, GTmetrix, Yoast
  • Prototyping and collaboration: InVision, Zeplin, Miro

Industries that hire freelance website designers

Website design is one of the most universally needed skills on Freelancer.com because every industry has an online presence to maintain. Common use cases include:

  • E-commerce and direct-to-consumer brands launching Shopify or WooCommerce stores
  • Professional services such as law firms, accountants, consultants, and clinics
  • Real estate agencies needing IDX listings and lead-capture pages
  • SaaS and tech startups building marketing sites and product landing pages
  • Restaurants, hotels, and hospitality brands requiring booking integrations
  • Non-profits, schools, and community organisations
  • Personal brands, coaches, authors, and creators building portfolio sites

How to evaluate a freelance website designer

The strongest signal is the portfolio. Look for live, working sites — not just static mockups — across industries similar to yours. Pay attention to typography, spacing, hierarchy, and how the design holds up on a phone. Consistency across multiple projects matters more than one standout piece.

Beyond visuals, check that the designer understands conversion principles, page speed, and basic SEO. Read client reviews for comments on responsiveness, deadline adherence, and willingness to revise. Verify proficiency with the specific platform you need, whether that's WordPress, Webflow, or Shopify.

Useful interview questions to ask candidates:

  • Can you walk me through your design process from brief to launch?
  • How do you approach mobile-first responsive design?
  • What steps do you take to optimise a site for Core Web Vitals and page speed?
  • How do you handle revisions and feedback rounds during a project?
  • Can you show me a past project where you improved a client's conversion rate or user experience?

Web design vs web development: knowing what you need

Web design focuses on the look, feel, layout, and user experience of a site. Web development covers the back-end functionality — databases, server logic, custom integrations, and complex applications. Many freelance website designers also handle front-end development and CMS setup, which is enough for most marketing sites and small stores. If you need custom user accounts, payment logic, or APIs, you may need a web developer alongside your designer.

What are the steps to hiring a freelance website designer?

Hiring the right website designer comes down to writing a clear brief, comparing proposals carefully, and verifying portfolio quality before you award the work. The process below is built for typical web design projects, from a small business site to a full e-commerce build.

Step 1: Post your project on Freelancer.com

The brief is the single biggest determinant of bid quality. A clear, specific project post filters out mismatched freelancers and attracts designers whose portfolio genuinely fits your project. Head to the post project page , fill in the brief, scope, timeline, and budget, and publish your project for designers to bid on.

  • Specify the platform you want (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, custom HTML)
  • List the number of pages or templates and any required functionality such as booking, e-commerce, or contact forms
  • Share two or three reference websites that capture the visual style you want
  • Describe your industry, target audience, and primary conversion goal
  • State whether you need copywriting, photography, or logo design included
  • Note technical requirements: hosting, domain, accessibility, multilingual support, integrations
  • Indicate your timeline and budget range so bids come in realistic

Step 2: Review bids from freelancers

Bids are short proposals, not just price quotes. Strong website designers will show they read your brief by referencing your industry, suggesting a platform if you haven't chosen one, and outlining a rough process. Use bid quality to shortlist three to five candidates worth deeper review.

  • Look for proposals that describe a clear design process, from wireframe to launch
  • Check whether the designer asks intelligent clarifying questions about content, branding, or functionality
  • Confirm proposed timelines are realistic for the scope and revision rounds you expect
  • Note whether the bid references relevant tools — Figma, Elementor, Webflow, Shopify themes
  • Look for sample work or portfolio links attached directly to the bid
  • Use Freelancer.com chat to ask follow-up questions and gauge communication style before shortlisting

Step 3: Evaluate freelancers and award the project

Final selection combines proposal quality with profile evidence. Look at portfolio depth, consistency across projects, and written reviews from past clients. For website design, live site links matter more than static mockups — visit them, test them on mobile, and check load speed.

  • Review portfolio breadth: multiple completed sites in styles and platforms relevant to your project
  • Check star ratings alongside the volume of completed projects, not just the average
  • Read written client reviews for mentions of responsiveness, revisions, and on-time delivery
  • Confirm verified payment method, completion rate, and on-time delivery rate on the profile
  • Visit live portfolio links and test them on mobile, tablet, and desktop
  • Award the project and use Freelancer.com Milestone Payments to release funds in stages — wireframes, design approval, development, and final launch — so you stay protected throughout the engagement

How long does a typical website design project take?

A simple five-to-seven page marketing site usually takes two to four weeks from brief to launch. Larger e-commerce stores, custom Webflow builds, or full redesigns with content migration commonly run six to twelve weeks. Timeline depends heavily on how quickly you can supply content, feedback, and approvals.

What's the difference between a website designer and a web developer?

A website designer focuses on visual design, user experience, and front-end layout, often using tools like Figma and platforms like WordPress or Webflow. A web developer writes the underlying code, builds custom functionality, and handles back-end systems. Many freelancers do both for standard sites, but complex applications usually need both specialists.

Do I need to provide content and images, or will the designer create them?

Most website designers expect you to supply core copy and brand assets, though many offer copywriting, stock photography sourcing, or custom illustration as add-on services. Be clear in your brief about what you can provide and what you need help with so bids reflect the full scope.

Can I hire a freelance website designer for a one-off landing page?

Yes. Single landing pages, campaign microsites, and one-page portfolios are common short-engagement projects on Freelancer.com. Many designers also offer ongoing maintenance, content updates, and iterative conversion optimisation after launch.

Will my new website be mobile-friendly and SEO-ready?

Any competent website designer will deliver a fully responsive site that works on phones, tablets, and desktops. Basic on-page SEO — clean URL structure, meta tags, heading hierarchy, image alt text, and fast load times — is standard. Deeper SEO strategy, keyword research, or content marketing usually falls to an SEO specialist.
